# Reacting to an incident

A synthetic page for the tests: a table whose cells hold sentences and lists, far wider than any window.

| Situation | Who acts | What happens | Checks |
|-----------|:---------|--------------|--------|
| **Detect.** An alert, a customer report or a failed deploy raises the first signal, often outside working hours and often with only half of the picture: one graph that looks wrong, or one message from somebody who cannot log in. | The engineer on call, with the duty lead as a second pair of eyes when the alert touches more than one service at the same time, which is when a single reader of dashboards tends to settle on the first plausible cause and stop looking. | The engineer on call confirms that the signal is real, notes the time, and writes one line in the shared channel so that nobody starts a second investigation in parallel. If it cannot be confirmed within ten minutes it is treated as real anyway, because a late start has cost far more in the past than a false alarm ever did. | - Alert acknowledged within five minutes<br>- First line posted in the shared channel<br>- Start time written down where everyone can see it<br>- Second reader called in when unsure<br>- Customers not yet told anything that is a guess |
| *Contain.* The cause is not known yet, but the harm is visible and growing: orders fail, pages time out, or data is being written that will have to be repaired by hand later on, one record after the other. | The duty lead decides and the service owner carries out the change. Nobody else touches production until the duty lead says so, however obvious the fix may look from outside, and nobody is asked for an estimate while they are typing. | Stop the harm before understanding it. Roll back the last release, switch the feature off or move traffic away, whichever is fastest and can be undone. Every action goes into the timeline with its time and its author, in plain words, as it happens. See the [rollback guide](https://example.org/handbook/rollback) and run `deploy --undo` rather than patching forward. | - Harm stopped or clearly shrinking<br>- Each step in the timeline with its time<br>- Customers told what is known, ~~not what is hoped~~<br>- Nothing changed that cannot be undone<br>- The next update promised for a stated time |
| Recover. The harm has stopped, the service runs in a reduced or patched state, and the people who held it together for the last hours are tired and would like to call it done and go home before anything else breaks. | The service owner, with whoever built the part that failed. The duty lead keeps the timeline and the channel, tells support what they may say, and sends home anyone who has been awake for too long to be trusted with production. | Bring the service back to its normal state step by step and watch each step for a quarter of an hour before taking the next one. Data that was written while the fault lasted is checked against a known good copy, and anything that a script cannot repair is listed by hand so that it is not forgotten once the pressure is gone. | - Service at its normal level for a full hour<br>- Damaged records counted and listed<br>- Support has a statement it can send<br>- Temporary switches and patches written down<br>- A date for removing each of them |
| Review. A few days have passed, the service is stable, and memories of who knew what at which moment have already begun to drift towards the story that sounds most reasonable afterwards, which is rarely the one that happened. | Everyone who took part, led by someone who did not. The reviewer's task is to ask how each decision looked at the time it was made, with what was known then, and to keep the talk away from who should have known better. | Within five working days the timeline is read aloud from the first signal to the last step, and for every gap the group asks what would have closed it sooner. The result is a short list of changes with one owner and one date each; anything longer is not read by anybody. The list is linked from the handbook and looked at again after a month. | - Review held within five working days<br>- At most five changes, each with one owner<br>- Dates agreed in the meeting, not afterwards<br>- Summary sent to everyone who was paged<br>- Follow-up in the calendar a month later |

The table above must stay readable at any width.
